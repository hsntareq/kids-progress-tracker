# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Children use the app to see quests, complete activities, earn points, track progress, and request rewards. Parents use the app to manage family activities, approve requests, and encourage sustained progress. Administrators may oversee multiple families and users.

The primary design priority is the child's experience: it should feel understandable, motivating, safe, and achievable from an age-appropriate, responsive interface.

## Product Purpose

Kids Progress Tracker turns everyday tasks and learning activities into visible progress for families. Success means children understand what to do next, complete tasks confidently, and feel recognized without pressure or confusing reward mechanics.

## Brand Personality

Playful, encouraging, adventurous, and calm. The interface should feel like a guided family quest rather than a competitive game or a dense productivity dashboard.

## Anti-references

- Gimmicky game interfaces with excessive animation or visual noise
- Manipulative streaks, scarcity, shame, or punitive reward mechanics
- Dense business-dashboard layouts that hide the child's next action
- Childish interfaces that do not also work for parents or administrators
- Decorative motion that delays or obscures task completion

## Design Principles

- Make the next action obvious: show the child's active quest and next step without extra navigation.
- Celebrate effort and progress, not only scores or competition.
- Keep family roles clear while giving children a larger, simpler working surface.
- Use graphical progress that communicates at a glance and remains understandable to children.
- Design for agency and safety: avoid accidental actions, expose approval states, and keep parent controls clear.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

- Target WCAG 2.2 AA for contrast, keyboard use, focus states, touch targets, and semantics.
- Preserve readable text and avoid color-only meaning.
- Support reduced-motion preferences and allow sound and animation to be non-essential.
- Use plain, encouraging language and avoid jargon in child-facing screens.
- Keep layouts usable on phones, tablets, laptops, and larger displays.
